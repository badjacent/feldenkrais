# The Feldenkrais Method

The Feldenkrais Method is a form of somatic education developed by Moshe Feldenkrais (1904--1984), an Israeli physicist, engineer, and martial artist. It uses gentle, exploratory movement to improve how you move, think, and feel -- not by building strength or flexibility, but by refining the nervous system's organization of action.

## How it works

A Feldenkrais lesson guides you through a sequence of movements, typically lasting 45--60 minutes. The movements are small, slow, and non-strenuous. You aren't trying to stretch a muscle or reach a position -- you're paying attention to *how* you do what you do: which parts of your body participate, where effort accumulates, what you can let go of.

The lessons follow a characteristic arc:
1. **Establish a baseline** -- Lie down (or sit, or stand) and notice how your body contacts the surface. This isn't a warmup; it's a reference point you'll return to.
2. **Introduce a movement** -- A simple action, like turning your head or lifting a hand. You do it many times, noticing what happens.
3. **Vary and differentiate** -- The same movement is explored from different angles, in different combinations, with different parts leading. You might turn your head with your eyes, then without; roll your pelvis one way while your shoulder goes the other.
4. **Integrate** -- Return to the baseline. Notice what changed. Often the differences are vivid: one side feels longer, heavier, more present.

The method has two modalities:
- **Awareness Through Movement (ATM)** -- Group lessons taught verbally (what this project analyzes). The teacher gives movement instructions; students follow at their own pace.
- **Functional Integration (FI)** -- One-on-one, hands-on sessions where the practitioner guides movement through touch.

## Why the lessons sound the way they do

Feldenkrais lessons share a distinctive verbal texture. You'll hear phrases like "notice the difference," "do it without effort," "lie on your back" in virtually every lesson. The teacher rarely demonstrates or corrects -- the instructions are designed to direct attention, not prescribe form. This makes the lessons rich for students but challenging for computational analysis, since the *generic* language can overwhelm the *specific* content of any given lesson.

## What this project does with it

This system transcribes and analyzes Feldenkrais ATM lessons to make them searchable, browsable, and understandable at a structural level. Each lesson goes through multiple analysis passes that extract:
- **Step-by-step segmentation** -- What movement is happening, in what position, at what point in the lesson
- **Biomechanical analysis** -- Body parts involved, movement chains, differentiation patterns
- **Linguistic analysis** -- Teaching strategies, imagery, attention directives
- **Concept extraction** -- What makes each lesson distinctive for search

The goal is to help practitioners and students find, compare, and study lessons by what they actually *do* -- not just by their titles or surface-level keywords.
