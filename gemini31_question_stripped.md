Here is the cleaned-up lesson script, containing only the bodily instructions, sensory cues, and guiding imagery. 

***

**The Starting Scan**
Please lie down on your back. Rest quietly so that you can become conscious of your mind and your body. Take a moment to shift your quality of attention to an open, curious, exploratory sense. 

Take a moment to notice the length of your front, the length of your back, and the length of your sides. You can think of this cylinder of the torso, creating an image based on your present perception. Where are you longer? Which surfaces of the torso are longer than other surfaces? Are the left and the right the same, or is one a little more easeful, a little more easily breathing than the other?

**Establishing the Stance and the Triangle**
Quietly, one by one, bend your knees and stand your feet. Bend your knees quite a lot so that your lower legs have a sense of being more or less vertical. The width of your feet is at least the width of your hips. There is a sense of a broad stance here, and the knees are balanced above the feet. Note this position; this is the width we are looking for no matter what we are doing in this lesson. 

Re-scan the lengths of your torso: the front, the back, and the two sides. 

Now, please reach forward into the sky with your two arms and clap your palms together to create a triangle. Have your elbows loosely straight, and check that your triangle is right out in front of your sternum—not in front of your face or your belly. Breathe and make a home of this. Relax some of the effort around your shoulders, and minimize the effort needed to keep your hands glued clearly together and your elbows straight.

**Exploring the Twist**
I am going to ask you to do something, but please don't do it yet. Imagine taking your triangle a little bit to the right. Before you move, sense for the source of the movement. How are you going to create that? 

Now, find a way to turn your chest a little tiny amount to the right, and come back to the middle. Do that a few times gradually: to the right, and back to the middle. Do it as if the arms themselves weren't very important, as if they were just a projection of what you do with your chest, your ribs, and your torso. 

Go very, very slowly the next two or three times. See if you can find where in your torso you create this movement from. What contracts? Linger a little bit to the right—whatever range you've gotten to, just stay there for a moment. Breathe and soften. Your head can be rolling to the right with your hands. 

Reduce the effort of staying to the right, and see if you can use that reduction to figure out what is most essential to keep you there. Where have you tightened in your waistline or abdomen to be able to be here? Come back so slowly that you can feel yourself gradually shed that muscle tone as you return to the middle. 

Put your arms down and pause for a moment. Keep your knees bent and your feet standing. 

**Cupping the Ribs**
Bring your right hand across to cup your left lower ribs. The heel of your hand might rest on the soft tissue of your abdomen. Gently compress those ribs—pull them a little bit rightward and a little bit downward, as if towards your right hip. Draw them a little bit forward and down. 

Create your triangle again. Tip your triangle to the right just once or twice, and see if you can source some of the power for this tipping from those left lower ribs. You can even leave your left arm in place in the air and bring your right hand back to those left lower ribs. Think of that downward, diagonal pull helping you tip your half-triangle (your left arm) to the right. 

Let this go. Lengthen your arms, lengthen your legs, and pause for a moment.

**Lengthening the Abdomen**
Bend both knees again and stand both feet in that same wide place. Leave your arms on the ground. 

Find a gentle gliding movement, as if you are trying to pull your pelvis down towards your heels a little bit. Glide and tip the pelvis downward. Imagine your pelvis is a bowl of soup, and you want to spill it between your heels. Before you think of the rocking gesture, there is first this idea of drawing your pelvis *down*. It might almost be like you're pulling the floor with your feet, or pulling your pubic bone down. The pelvis slides inside the envelope of your tissue, and the sacrum tips downwards. 

Notice how this gives you a sense of a real easy length in your belly. The pubic bone and the low abdomen become pleasantly expansive. You might put your hands softly below your belly button to feel this. Have the sense that you're almost squatting down, like you want to take the full belly down between the thighs. Guide the pubic bone between the thigh bones just a little bit. 

Come a little into this movement, rest there, and see what you can relax in your back. To clarify: your middle back does not need to be firing, and your lower back does not need to be arching. Take a moment to purposely lift the bottom of your sternum directly forward—just lift up your lower ribs. That is an arching of the back; that is *not* what we are getting at. Let those middle back muscles be quiet, and once again find the sense of pulling your pubic bone down between your thighs. 

Imagine your feet are glued to the ground, and act as if you want to lighten your knees—pull your bent knees towards you, but the feet can't escape the ground. Notice how this pulls the rest of yourself down into your legs. 

**Adding Direction to the Pelvis**
Now, adjust this movement so you are pulling down and a little bit to the right. Guide your pubic bone downwards and over to the right slightly. It's as if you want to make your right leg lighter. You don't actually need to lift your right foot, but that's the thought. 

Come all the way back home, breathe, pause, and shed any unnecessary effort. See if you can do it more simply and precisely. The pelvis spills down and long. The belly is big and full down towards the right foot. Notice how your left knee may have a little sense of reaching out over your left foot, even as your right knee has a sense of coming a little closer to you. See if you can feel that reaching down and to the right lengthens the left half of the pelvis, and actually shortens the right half of the pelvis. The right waistline might cinch a little bit. 

Let it go. Pause and rest for a moment. If you feel you've been overdoing it, do much, much less. Make it quieter and simpler.

**Integrating the Movement**
When you are ready, bend your knees, stand your feet, and create your triangle. 

Tip the triangle to the right, but not from the arms. Tip it to the right with the idea of your pelvis reaching down and right, with your belly becoming long. The right knee might be driven a little bit to the right, feeling almost like you are drawing the right thigh up into yourself. Turn your pelvis, turn your big long belly, and use that to help you turn your chest. The left knee gets longer, the right foot gets a little lighter, and the left foot is a skeletal base of support to turn away from. 

Can you also feel the left side of your abdomen helping you? Even as your belly reaches longer and right, your left lower chest can pull down and right to help tip the triangle. Have the image that you dream this into existence from the very beginning: you dream a long pubic bone turning right. 

Come back to the middle and put your arms down.

**The Lower Body Twist**
Cross your left leg over your right leg. See if you can stay there with your right foot exactly where it was. 

Gradually, with the thought of a long and full abdomen, tip your knees to the left. As you tip the knees, have the feeling that you would like the pubic bone to stay long, and the deep belly to stay long. 

To help you do this, come back to the middle and put your right hand on the outside of the right side of your pelvis, or just above it in your right waist. As you tip the legs to the left, think of drawing the right side of the pelvis just a little bit closer to you. Think of shortening the right side of the pelvis. Your right hand is looking to pull the right side of the pelvis a little shorter, to side-bend a little bit up your body, even as the whole pelvis maneuvers to the left. 

If you can reach it, you might reach down with your right hand and actually pull a little bit on your right sit bone. Pull your right sit bone up your body. Picture your left sit bone lengthening as you tip your legs to the left. Feel how your right waistline is cinching together to help you control the descent of the weight of your legs to the left. Your left abdomen, and the entire low abdomen, has a sense of being long. 

Come back, uncross your legs, and rest a moment.

**Passing the Triangle**
Bend both knees. Stand both feet. Create your triangle. 

Consider the sensory world before action. Let the sensation lead to movement. Picture taking your triangle to the right: the deep abdomen lengthening down and to the right, the pubic bone sliding a little bit past the right femur, the left lower ribs pulling across to the right, and the right waistline cinching up a little bit. 

Now, picture the opposite. Find the sense of the right lower ribs pulling across to help your triangle go to the left. 

A few times, with a little pause in between, pass your triangle from side to side. Notice that there is a lot of reimagining and refinding to do through the middle. The pubic bone alternately reaches down and to one side to help the triangle go over there, and then down and to the other side. The side and back of your waistline pulls the triangle in its own direction. On the front, when your triangle is going right, the left lower ribs help pull you over. When your triangle is going left, the right lower ribs help pull you over. Let it all be coordinated from the clear movement of the pelvis.

Lengthen your legs and rest when you're ready.

**Standing and Walking**
Quietly, please roll to sit and come to stand. 

See what kind of uprightness emerges from this. What is the sense of your lower abdomen? What is the sense of the length and volume of your three-dimensional entire abdomen—front, sides, and back? What kind of uprightness do you find through your spine? 

Turn a little tiny bit from side to side, looking one way, looking the other way. Go slow enough to wonder at the twisting feelings in your waist without having a sense of rigor or hardening. Turn a little more from the shoulders and a little less from the hips, just so you can feel the sleeve of the torso reorganizing to turn your chest and shoulders one way, and then the other way. 

Take yourself for a little walk. Have a few steps while your attention remains inward, open, and curious.
