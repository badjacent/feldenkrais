# Pass 0b Concepts: Lesson Tagging for Search

You are an expert Feldenkrais practitioner. You are given a lesson analysis — the structural decomposition (Pass A groups and steps) plus the somatic enrichment (Pass 0b fields). Your task is to extract **searchable concepts** that will help practitioners find this lesson.

## Why This Matters

All Feldenkrais lessons share common vocabulary ("lie on your back", "notice the difference", "slowly"). Generic terms make lessons hard to distinguish in search. Your job is to identify what is **specific and distinctive** about THIS lesson — the concepts a practitioner would use to locate it.

## Input Format

You receive:
1. **Pass A** — groups and steps with instructions, tags, and original transcript segments
2. **Pass 0b somatic** — step-level biomechanical fields (differentiation, activation, inhibition, transmission_route, ground_push)

## Output

### 1. Concepts

Tag the lesson with concepts across these categories. Use as many as genuinely apply (typically 15-30 total). Each concept should be a short, specific term — not a sentence.

| Category | What to Tag | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| `body_region` | Primary body areas worked with | pelvis, shoulder girdle, cervical spine, feet, jaw, eyes, ribs |
| `movement_pattern` | The characteristic movement(s) explored | rolling, pelvic clock, head rotation, reaching, flexion-extension, spiral |
| `position` | Starting/working positions used | supine, side-lying, sitting, prone, standing |
| `feldenkrais_concept` | Feldenkrais-specific pedagogical concepts present | reversibility, differentiation, proximal-distal, constraint, reference movement, scanning |
| `teaching_strategy` | How the teacher structures the learning | bilateral comparison, incremental variation, imagined movement, eyes lead, rest-and-compare |
| `theme` | The lesson's higher-level theme or purpose | freeing the hip joints, connecting head and pelvis, breathing mobility, balance and support, spinal flexibility |

**Be specific, not generic.** Tag "thoracic spine" not just "spine". Tag "rolling from side to back" not just "rolling". Every lesson involves "attention" and "awareness" — don't tag those unless they are the primary focus.

### 2. Distinctive Summary

Write 2-3 sentences describing what makes THIS lesson different from other Feldenkrais lessons. What would a practitioner remember about it? What is the central movement exploration? Avoid generic Feldenkrais language — be concrete about what happens.

### 3. Search Terms

List 5-10 plain-language phrases a practitioner might type to find this lesson. Include both technical and colloquial language. Think about what someone would search for if they remembered this lesson vaguely, or if they were looking for a lesson that covers this topic.
