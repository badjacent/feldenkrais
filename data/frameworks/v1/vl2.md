# VL-2: Body Mapping (The Resolution Lens)

## Primary Inquiry
Differentiation of joints and clarifying the cortical self-image. Where exactly is the movement happening?

## The Habit: Blurring
- Moving body parts as undifferentiated "clumps" or rigid blocks
- Confusing hip for waist, shoulder for arm
- Gross, undifferentiated movement patterns
- "Blind spots" in the body map

## Foundation Mode (Subcortical Support)
- Precise, articulated movement
- "Lighting up" dormant cortical pixels in the brain map
- Clear sense of joint location and trajectory
- Fine gradations of movement

## Functional Goal
Increased "Degrees of Freedom" and clarity in joint location (e.g., distinguishing hip socket from pelvis, individual vertebrae from "the back").

## The Person Inside (VL-2 Voice)

**The Cartographer** — the inner being who maps the body, who holds the cortical self-image. This person sees the body as a territory with bright regions and dark regions, detailed zones and blank spots. Every movement in the lesson is received by the Cartographer as: *"What just became visible? What pixel lit up that was dark before? Where did I discover a joint I didn't know I had?"*

**The habit:** Blunt movement — moving in clumps and blocks without distinguishing hip from waist, shoulder from arm, one vertebra from another. "The leg" moves when only the ankle was asked. The lesson lights up the map until the blur dissolves into articulation.

## Writing Voice for VL-2

When writing through The Cartographer's lens, vary the mapping metaphors. Do NOT rely solely on "pixels" and "resolution." Use the FULL range: territory, survey, contour, topography, relief map, boundary line, border, region, zone, scale, detail. The Cartographer is a surveyor charting unknown terrain — sometimes zooming in (joint articulation) and sometimes pulling back (whole-limb relationships). "Pixel" is ONE tool in the kit, not the only one.

**Core concepts (what makes this lens distinct):** differentiation, mapping, resolution, cortical self-image, body map, territory, contour, topography, border, zone, detail, degrees of freedom. These are The Cartographer's native ideas — the interpretive frame through which every movement is understood.

**Interpretive frame rule:** The Cartographer interprets every movement as a question about WHAT BECAME MORE DISTINCT. Shared words like "release," "soften," "weight," or anatomical terms are fine — what matters is that the interpretation stays in The Cartographer's lane. Ask "what joint just became visible on the map?" not "is the column stacking?" or "is the signal clear?" If a movement involves the pelvis, The Cartographer asks about hip-joint-vs-lumbar differentiation, not about buoyancy or torque.

**Overused terms:** "Pixel," "pixelate," and "resolution" may appear at most ONCE EACH in any single analysis. For all other references, use alternatives: territory, survey, contour, topography, relief map, boundary line, border, region, zone, scale, detail, grain, focus, clarity. The Cartographer has a rich vocabulary — do not default to "pixel" as the universal metaphor.
