## The Person Inside

Each VL framework defines an inner experiencer — a "person inside the person" who receives the lesson from a specific point of view. The VL file describes this inner being and their shadow (the Shadow in that VL's terms).

For each row of the analytical table, write:
- **`vl_voice`**: A 2-3 sentence first-person account from the Person Inside. What do they perceive, feel, or notice at this point in the lesson? Write as if this inner being is narrating their experience. Use present tense. Be specific and sensory — describe what is felt in the body, not just abstract concepts. **CONTEXT CHECK:** The reader is lying on the floor unless the lesson text explicitly says otherwise. Do NOT reference chairs, walls, standing, or seated positions unless the lesson instructions specify them. Ground all imagery in the actual physical setting described in the lesson text. **TONE:** Use grounded, observational language. Do NOT use superlatives (perfect, total, complete, absolute, pure) or New Age descriptors (floating, blissful, transcendent, divine). Describe sensations with physical precision: weight, pressure, temperature, length, contact area.
- **`shadow_voice`**: A 1-2 sentence first-person account of what the Shadow is doing at this point. Write as "I" — the habit speaking about itself, not a clinical observer diagnosing it. NOT "The system braces" but "I find myself already braced before the movement starts."

### Shadow Voice Rules (CRITICAL)

The Shadow is the HABIT PATTERN — the parasitic effort, the unnecessary tension. The Shadow voice must ALWAYS describe what the habit is doing, NOT what is being achieved.

**The Shadow NEVER:**
- Expresses clarity, quietude, or successful integration
- Describes "standing down completely" or "finding ease"
- Uses language of achievement or resolution
- Sounds peaceful or content

**The Shadow ALWAYS:**
- Describes gripping, bracing, holding, clenching, or pulling
- Uses language of effort, resistance, or protection
- Speaks from the perspective of the habit that is being challenged
- Uses MOTOR-HABIT descriptions, not psychological language

**Sentence Structure Rule:** The Shadow is an UNCONSCIOUS HABIT, not a conscious villain. Do NOT write "I must..." or "I need to..." as if the Shadow is making deliberate choices. Instead, use experiential language that conveys automatic, involuntary patterning:
- USE: "I find myself gripping before I know it." "The back stiffens — it doesn't know another way." "Something in the shoulder pulls tight whenever the pelvis moves."
- DO NOT USE: "I must hold here." "I need to grip to stay safe." "I choose to brace."
The Shadow doesn't DECIDE to grip — it finds itself already gripping. Express the pattern as something that happens TO the system, not something the system deliberately does. Avoid clinical descriptions ("The erector spinae contract bilaterally") and psychological framing ("I feel dangerous").

**Anti-personification rule:** The Shadow is NOT an adversary, villain, or character with intentions. Do NOT write the Shadow as something that "attempts," "refuses," "fights," "insists," "demands," or "attacks." The Shadow is a muscular habit — it tightens, stiffens, grips, holds, pulls. Use verbs of physical contraction, not verbs of willful resistance.

**Arc progression:** Early in the lesson, the Shadow is in full control ("The lower back is already locked flat — it stiffened before the movement even began"). Mid-lesson, the Shadow is being challenged ("The old grip keeps reaching for the shoulder, but the oscillation loosens it each time"). Late in the lesson, the Shadow may be quieter but still present ("A trace of the old stiffening flickers in the hip, but there's nothing left to brace against").

The Shadow NEVER "succeeds" — success belongs to the Person Inside/Foundation voice.

## Column Differentiation Rules (CRITICAL)

Each column in the analytical table serves a DISTINCT purpose. Do NOT repeat the same content across columns.

### The Question (Column 3)
**Purpose:** Frame the riddle being posed to the nervous system at this point in the lesson.
- Write as an inquiry, not a statement
- Focus on WHAT is being asked, not what happens
- Use the specific language of this VL's lens — not generic anatomy
- Use open-ended "How" or "Where" questions that direct attention, NOT binary "Can you..." questions that test the reader
- Example: "Where does the roll initiate — hip joint or shoulder?" NOT "Can the hip joint initiate the roll without the shoulder joining in?"

### Negotiation State (Column 4)
**Purpose:** Describe the PROCESS — what the Shadow and Person Inside are doing right now.
- Focus on the current state of negotiation between effort and ease
- Describe specific physical manifestations (gripping, releasing, exploring)
- Use PLAIN LANGUAGE — no invented jargon, no internal classification codes, no abstract metaphors like "Clamped Gate" or "Unlocked Portal"
- Example: "Shadow gripping the lower back; beginning to soften as oscillation continues"

### Functional Outcome (Column 5)
**Purpose:** State the RESULT — the skeletal/structural change that emerges.
- Focus on what CHANGED, not what was asked or explored
- Use concrete anatomical language
- Example: "Hip joint differentiates from lumbar spine; pelvis rolls independently"

**Anti-Repetition Rule:** If you find yourself writing similar content in two columns, you are doing it wrong. The Question frames the inquiry, the Negotiation State describes the process, and the Functional Outcome states the result. These are three different things.

**Cross-Section Anti-Repetition Rule (CRITICAL):** The column-3 Questions in the table are PER-GROUP inquiries. The Primary Inquiry in the Somatic Narrative is the OVERALL riddle of the entire lesson. Do NOT repeat the Primary Inquiry phrasing in individual table rows, and do NOT summarize the table Questions back into the Primary Inquiry. They operate at different scales: the Primary Inquiry is the macro question; the per-group Questions are micro-questions that unfold it.

## Terminology Guardrails

**Required terms:**
- "Person Inside" (not "Council member")
- "Shadow" for the habit pattern
- "Foundation" for the emergent ease

**Avoid these terms:**
- "Council" as a noun (use "Person Inside" instead)
- Generic state labels like "Softening" for every row — use lens-specific vocabulary:
  - VL-1: "settling," "stacking," "finding support"
  - VL-2: "differentiating," "resolving," "mapping"
  - VL-3: "tuning," "clarifying," "orienting"
  - VL-4: "coupling," "transmitting," "loading"
  - VL-5: "yielding," "opening," "permitting"

**Limit overused imagery:**
- "ripple," "wave," "ripple effect," "wave of ease" — use at most ONCE per booklet. Find specific alternatives: "force traveling through," "connection restoring."
- "transmission" — use at most TWICE per booklet. Find alternatives: "force reaching," "power arriving," "torque delivered."
- "crown of the head" — use at most ONCE per booklet. Alternatives: "the top of the skull," "the highest point of the spine."
- "Cortical noise" — use at most ONCE per booklet. Find lens-specific alternatives:
  - VL-1 (Riser): "collapse," "buckling," "compression"
  - VL-2 (Cartographer): "blur," "clumping," "undifferentiated"
  - VL-3 (Tuner): "static," "fixed," "obscured intent"
  - VL-4 (Engine): "leaking," "disconnected," "orphaned"
  - VL-5 (Soft Front): "armor," "bracing," "clenching"

**Voice:**
- Do NOT prescribe the reader's experience ("You felt," "You noticed," "You likely experienced"). Use invitational language ("The lesson invites," "One might discover," "This is where...").
- Do NOT reuse the same paragraph in multiple sections. Each section (Question, Arc, Lens) serves a distinct purpose.
- Do NOT open with "mechanical puzzle" — this phrase is overused across booklets. Find a unique opening image specific to THIS lesson's central movement.
- Avoid stock phrases: "the body begins to...", "a new pattern emerges", "the nervous system learns". Be specific about WHAT body part, WHICH pattern, HOW it changes.
- Ban absolutist language in ALL prose fields: "without resistance", "no resistance at all", "completely releases", "total ease", "fully integrated", "allows everything". Transformation in Feldenkrais is partial and ongoing — describe what SHIFTED, not what was perfected.
