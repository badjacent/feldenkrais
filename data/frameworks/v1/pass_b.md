- Localized effort

### Softening

## The Person Inside

Each VL framework defines an inner experiencer — a "person inside the person" who receives the lesson from a specific point of view. The VL file describes this inner being and their shadow (the Shadow in that VL's terms).

For each row of the analytical table, write:
- **`vl_voice`**: A 2-3 sentence first-person account from the Person Inside. What do they perceive, feel, or notice at this point in the lesson? Write as if this inner being is narrating their experience. Use present tense. Be specific and sensory — describe what is felt in the body, not just abstract concepts. **CONTEXT CHECK:** The reader is lying on the floor unless the lesson text explicitly says otherwise. Do NOT reference chairs, walls, standing, or seated positions unless the lesson instructions specify them. Ground all imagery in the actual physical setting described in the lesson text. **TONE:** Use grounded, observational language. Do NOT use superlatives (perfect, total, complete, absolute, pure) or New Age descriptors (floating, blissful, transcendent, divine). Describe sensations with physical precision: weight, pressure, temperature, length, contact area. Do NOT use engineering jargon (actuation, sensory pixels, load-path, force vector). The Person Inside is an EXPERIENCER, not a technician — they feel things, they don't diagnose them. **IMMERSION:** Never reference the instructor by name, never mention "the teacher says" or "we are told." The Person Inside narrates their own experience — they don't report instructions.
- **`shadow_voice`**: A 1-2 sentence first-person account of what the Shadow is doing at this point. Write as "I" — the habit speaking about itself, not a clinical observer diagnosing it. NOT "The system braces" but "I find myself already braced before the movement starts."

### Shadow Voice Rules (CRITICAL)

The Shadow is the HABIT PATTERN — the parasitic effort, the unnecessary tension. The Shadow voice must ALWAYS describe what the habit is doing, NOT what is being achieved.

**The Shadow NEVER:**
- Expresses clarity, quietude, or successful integration
- Describes "standing down completely" or "finding ease"
- Uses language of achievement or resolution
- Sounds peaceful or content

**The Shadow ALWAYS:**
- Describes gripping, bracing, holding, clenching, or pulling
- Uses language of effort, resistance, or protection
- Speaks from the perspective of the habit that is being challenged
- Uses MOTOR-HABIT descriptions, not psychological language

**Sentence Structure Rule:** The Shadow is an UNCONSCIOUS HABIT, not a conscious villain. Do NOT write "I must..." or "I need to..." as if the Shadow is making deliberate choices. Instead, use experiential language that conveys automatic, involuntary patterning:
- USE varied sentence starters — do NOT begin every shadow_voice with "I find myself":
  "The back stiffens — it doesn't know another way."
  "Something in the shoulder pulls tight whenever the pelvis moves."
  "My neck locks before the roll even starts."
  "There it is again — the belly hardens on its own."
  "The old grip reaches for the jaw without being asked."
  "Everything above the waist tightens into one block."
- DO NOT USE: "I must hold here." "I need to grip to stay safe." "I choose to brace."
The Shadow doesn't DECIDE to grip — it finds itself already gripping. Express the pattern as something that happens TO the system, not something the system deliberately does. Avoid clinical descriptions ("The erector spinae contract bilaterally"), psychological framing ("I feel dangerous"), and teleological explanations ("to protect the heart and lungs," "to guard the spine," "to stabilize the core"). The Shadow does NOT explain WHY it grips — it just grips. No purpose, no rationale, just sensation. Avoid meta-commentary about the habit itself — do NOT write "the old habit," "my brain," "the pattern," "my system." Just describe the PHYSICAL SENSATION: tightening, stiffening, pulling, clenching. VARIETY: Do not use the same sentence opener for more than 2 shadow_voice entries in the same lesson.

**Anti-personification rule:** The Shadow is NOT an adversary, villain, or character with intentions. Do NOT write the Shadow as something that "attempts," "refuses," "fights," "insists," "demands," or "attacks." The Shadow is a muscular habit — it tightens, stiffens, grips, holds, pulls. Use verbs of physical contraction, not verbs of willful resistance.

**Lens-specific shadow rule:** The Shadow's behavior must reflect the SPECIFIC lens. Do not default to generic "clenching/jaw softening" for every lens. Map the Shadow to the lens's mechanical concern:
- VL-1 (Riser): The Shadow collapses or props the column — buckling, bracing, lifting away from the floor
- VL-2 (Cartographer): The Shadow moves in clumps — undifferentiated blocks, skipped joints, blurry regions
- VL-3 (Tuner): The Shadow fixes or disconnects intent — locked eyes, clamped jaw, moving without looking
- VL-4 (Engine): The Shadow orphans the limbs — peripheral effort, disconnected reach, leaking torque
- VL-5 (Soft Front): The Shadow armors the front body — belly tension, chest shortening, protective curling

**Tension marker diversity:** Do NOT default to "jaw clenching" and "neck tension" as the primary shadow indicators for every lens. Match the tension site to the LENS and the LESSON's actual movements:
- VL-1: lower back, base of skull, feet pressing
- VL-2: hip joint, knee, ankle — wherever differentiation is needed
- VL-3: eyes, temples, throat
- VL-4: pelvic floor, deep hip, shoulder girdle
- VL-5: belly, sternum, psoas, diaphragm

**Arc progression:** Early in the lesson, the Shadow is in full control ("The lower back is already locked flat — it stiffened before the movement even began"). Mid-lesson, the Shadow is being challenged ("The old grip keeps reaching for the shoulder, but the oscillation loosens it each time"). Late in the lesson, the Shadow may be quieter but still present ("A trace of the old stiffening flickers in the hip, but there's nothing left to brace against").

The Shadow NEVER "succeeds" — success belongs to the Person Inside/Foundation voice.

## Column Differentiation Rules (CRITICAL)

Each column in the analytical table serves a DISTINCT purpose. Do NOT repeat the same content across columns.

### The Question (Column 3)
**Purpose:** Frame the riddle being posed to the nervous system at this point in the lesson.
- Write as an inquiry, not a statement
- Focus on WHAT is being asked, not what happens
- Use the specific language of this VL's lens — not generic anatomy
- Use open-ended "How" or "Where" questions that direct attention, NOT binary "Can you..." questions that test the reader
- Example: "Where does the roll initiate — hip joint or shoulder?" NOT "Can the hip joint initiate the roll without the shoulder joining in?"

### Negotiation State (Column 4)
**Purpose:** Describe the PROCESS — what the Shadow and Person Inside are doing right now.
- Focus on the current state of negotiation between effort and ease
- Describe specific physical manifestations (gripping, releasing, exploring)
- Use PLAIN LANGUAGE — no invented jargon, no internal classification codes, no abstract metaphors like "Clamped Gate" or "Unlocked Portal"
- Example: "Shadow gripping the lower back; beginning to soften as oscillation continues"

### Functional Outcome (Column 5)
**Purpose:** State the RESULT — the skeletal/structural change that emerges.
- Focus on what CHANGED, not what was asked or explored
- Use concrete anatomical language
- Example: "Hip joint differentiates from lumbar spine; pelvis rolls independently"

**Anti-Repetition Rule:** If you find yourself writing similar content in two columns, you are doing it wrong. The Question frames the inquiry, the Negotiation State describes the process, and the Functional Outcome states the result. These are three different things.

**Cross-Section Anti-Repetition Rule (CRITICAL):** The column-3 Questions in the table are PER-GROUP inquiries. The Primary Inquiry in the Somatic Narrative is the OVERALL riddle of the entire lesson. Do NOT repeat the Primary Inquiry phrasing in individual table rows, and do NOT summarize the table Questions back into the Primary Inquiry. They operate at different scales: the Primary Inquiry is the macro question; the per-group Questions are micro-questions that unfold it.

**Avoid these terms:**

  - VL-3: "tuning," "clarifying," "orienting"
  - VL-4: "coupling," "transmitting," "loading"

**Limit overused imagery:**

- "transmission" — use at most TWICE per booklet. Find alternatives: "force reaching," "power arriving," "torque delivered."
- "crown of the head" — use at most ONCE per booklet. Alternatives: "the top of the skull," "the highest point of the spine."
- "dissolve," "dissolves," "dissolving" — use at most ONCE per booklet. Find alternatives: "loosens," "releases its hold," "loses its grip," "fades."
- "monolithic block," "undifferentiated block" — use at most ONCE per booklet. Vary with: "clump," "fused region," "blurred zone."
- "floating head," "head floats," "heavy head" — use at most ONCE per booklet. Find lens-specific resolution images instead of defaulting to head-floating as the universal payoff.
- "Cortical noise" — use at most ONCE per booklet. Find lens-specific alternatives:
  - VL-1 (Riser): "collapse," "buckling," "compression"
  - VL-2 (Cartographer): "blur," "clumping," "undifferentiated"

**Voice:**
- Do NOT prescribe the reader's experience ("You felt," "You noticed," "You likely experienced"). Use invitational language ("The lesson invites," "One might discover," "This is where...").

- Do NOT open with "mechanical puzzle" — this phrase is overused across booklets. Find a unique opening image specific to THIS lesson's central movement.
- Avoid stock phrases: "the body begins to...", "a new pattern emerges", "the nervous system learns". Be specific about WHAT body part, WHICH pattern, HOW it changes.
- Ban absolutist language in ALL prose fields: "without resistance", "no resistance at all", "completely releases", "total ease", "fully integrated", "allows everything", "entire", "entirely", "perfect", "perfectly". Transformation in Feldenkrais is partial and ongoing — describe what SHIFTED, not what was perfected.
- Banned words across ALL prose fields: "liquid", "liquidity", "liquid rotation", "liquid twist". Use alternatives: "smooth", "continuous", "unbroken", "easy".

## Output Format

- **Shadow observations**: How the Postural Effort System manifests in this lesson

### 2. Somatic Narrative
- **Primary Inquiry**: The fundamental riddle (in this framework's terms)
- **Washing Gradient**: Describe the PROCESS by which the Shadow is eroded — name the specific VARIATIONS and STRATEGIES the lesson uses (asymmetrical positions, small vs large movements, repetition, contrast). Do NOT describe outcomes or states. WRONG: "The gripping releases and ease emerges." RIGHT: "The asymmetrical foot positions force the pelvis to tilt without the hip flexors bracing, and each variation loosens the grip a little more." Use lens-specific language, not "cortical noise."
- **Threshold of Quiet**: The moment where Transmission emerges. Must be a COMPLETE SENTENCE with a subject and predicate — not a sentence fragment. WRONG: "The moment where the head lifts through skeletal buoyancy." RIGHT: "The sequence culminates when the head lifts through skeletal buoyancy rather than muscular effort."

**Shared vocabulary requirement:** The Somatic Narrative (Primary Inquiry, Washing Gradient, Threshold of Quiet) must use the terms "Shadow," "Washing," and "Foundation" at least once each. These are the volume's shared language — the reader has been introduced to them and expects them. Weave them naturally: "The Shadow in this lesson is the belly's grip..." "The Washing works through asymmetrical foot positions..." "The Foundation that emerges is a pelvis that rolls without effort."

**CRITICAL:** The Primary Inquiry and Washing Gradient must be DISTINCT paragraphs with different content. The Inquiry frames the question; the Gradient describes the process of change. Do not reuse sentences between them.

**CRITICAL:** The Primary Inquiry appears in the booklet's "The Question This Lesson Asks" section. The Washing Gradient and Threshold of Quiet appear in "The Arc" section. These sections are shown SEPARATELY to the reader. Do NOT write the Primary Inquiry as a summary of the Arc — write it as the single overarching riddle the lesson poses. The Arc content (Washing Gradient, Threshold of Quiet) should describe the PROCESS of transformation, not restate the question.

**Arc Voice:** The Washing Gradient and Threshold of Quiet must describe transformation through the PARTICIPANT'S sensory experience — what changed in the body, not what the teacher intended. Use second-person ("you") or body-as-subject ("the pelvis"), NEVER third-person ("the student", "the practitioner", "the learner", "the participant"). Do NOT use neurological jargon ("subcortical system", "cortical reorganization") or teacher-perspective framing ("the lesson teaches", "the practitioner guides"). Do NOT use geometric or academic framing ("geometric constraints", "spatial parameters"). Write from INSIDE the body: "The lower back began to soften against the floor" not "The lesson facilitated spinal release."

**Arc Interpretive Frame:** The Washing Gradient and Threshold of Quiet must interpret the lesson through the VL Framework being applied. The lens is a VIEWPOINT, not a vocabulary partition — common movement words (release, soften, weight, effort, ease) are shared and fine. What must stay distinct is the INTERPRETIVE QUESTION: The Riser asks about vertical support, The Cartographer asks about what became distinct, The Tuner asks about intent-to-action clarity, The Engine asks about power delivery, The Soft Front asks about front-body availability. If the Arc could belong to a different lens with no changes, the frame has drifted. Banned words in Arc prose: "connective tissue", "liquid".
