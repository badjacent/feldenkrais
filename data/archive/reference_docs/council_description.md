The Council of Five: A Practitioner's Guide to the Persons Within
Most of the time, we think of our "self" as a single pilot sitting behind our eyes, pulling levers to move a mechanical body. But when you lie down for a Feldenkrais lesson, that model starts to fall apart. You realize that "you" aren't always the one in charge. Sometimes your neck won't let go, sometimes your ribs feel like a solid block, and sometimes—for a few fleeting moments—movement feels so effortless it’s as if the body is moving itself.

The Council of Five is a way of making sense of this. Instead of treating the body as a collection of muscles and bones, we treat it as a group of five distinct "people" living inside us. Each of these agents has its own job, its own personality, and its own "viewpoint" on what we are doing.

The Problem: The Shadow

We usually live under the thumb of a sixth character: The Shadow. This is our ego, the one who wants to do the lesson "right," who pushes through pain, and who treats the body like a tool to be used. The goal of a lesson is to "wash away" the Shadow’s dominance so the Council of Five can actually do their jobs.

The Council Members

When the Shadow steps back, we can finally hear the five specialists who actually know how to run a human being:

The Riser: The ancient navigator. They don't care about "looking good"; they only care about how the skeleton meets the floor to create height.

The Cartographer: The obsessive map-maker. They live for the tiny details—the exact way a finger joints or a rib slides.

The Aligner (Steering/Tone): The quiet guardian of the horizon. They manage the eyes and the jaw, keeping the "internal climate" calm and ready.

The Bridge (Core and Reach): The diplomat. They ensure the powerful center of the body supports the delicate reach of the hands.

The Guardian (Frontal Line): The protector. They decide when it’s safe to open up to the world and when we need to curl in for safety.

The Goal: From Bracing to Transmission

By thinking of these as "people," we change our relationship with ourselves. We stop "correcting" our posture and start negotiating with our Council.

We move from Bracing (the Shadow trying to force things) through a phase of Softening (listening to the Council’s feedback), until we reach Transmission—that magical state where the Council is in total harmony and movement becomes a clear, skeletal statement of intent.

Why this works for us (the students):

It turns a "lesson" into a meeting. If your shoulder is tight, you don't just stretch it; you ask the Guardian why they feel the need to protect you right now. If your movement is jerky, you ask the Cartographer to help you find the "missing pixels" in your map.

We aren't fixing a machine; we are listening to a Council.
