# Baby Rolls with Flexion

[Initial Scan and Head Rolling]
  1. Settle into the floor and scan your body contact, specifically the low back arch and shoulder blades.
  2. Observe the breath and roll the head gently from side to side.

[Flexing the Back on the Side]
  3. Roll onto the right side and organize for comfort with the right arm under the head and left hand in front.
  4. Move the back toward the wall behind you (flexing) and return to neutral.
  5. Leading with the left shoulder, twist the spine slightly back and return; then pause.
  6. Initiate from between the shoulder blades to move the back toward the wall, softening the chest and letting the chin come to the chest.
  7. Hold behind the head with the left hand; take the back back, letting the hand encourage the chin to the chest to lengthen the spine.
  8. Rest on the side or back and observe changes in breath and contact.

[Breath and Knee-to-Chest Relationships]
  9. Return to the right side; coordinate taking the back back with inhalation and then with exhalation to find support.
  10. Rest and observe breath expansion.
  11. On the right side, hold the left knee with the left hand; bring the knee toward the chest as the back moves back.
  12. Hold the left knee with the left hand and the head with the right hand; round the whole spine together by bringing the head and knee toward each other.
  13. Rest on the back and scan the increased contact and length of the spine.

[Lightness in the Leg and Rolling to the Back]
  14. On the right side, lift the left leg away from the right, finding a trajectory that makes the leg feel lighter (moving back and toward the chest).
  15. Rest and sense the expansion of the ribs.
  16. Holding the left knee, imagine kissing the knee or touching it with your forehead; roll back enough that gravity invites you toward the back.
  17. Continue the movement of lifting the knee and rounding the back until you roll completely onto your back.
  18. Rest on the back and compare the left and right sides.

[The Ball: Rolling with Forearms and Knees]
  19. Roll between the right side and the back, noticing the new organization of curling up.
  20. Bring forearms together in front of the face; lift the left leg and left arm together to roll onto the back.
  21. Continue rolling like a ball from side to side, softening the center and following the weight of the pelvis.

[Baby Rolls: Visual Interest and Feet]
  22. Roll while looking at the hands (baby imagery), then roll while looking toward the feet/toes.
  23. Rest on the back and scan the spine and shoulder blades.

[Pill Bug Rolling and Final Integration]
  24. Hold knees over chest, arms in front; roll from side to side like a pill bug, staying soft and connected.
  25. Final scan in lying: compare the low back arch and head roll to the beginning.
  26. Roll to sit, stand, and walk; notice the length of the back.
