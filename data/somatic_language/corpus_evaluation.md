# Corpus Evaluation: Seed Theory vs. 19 Lessons

Evaluated 19 lessons across 7 speaker/source groups against the seed theory's 8 mechanisms, 3 compositional patterns, and 5 anti-patterns. Each lesson was loaded from the database and analyzed for evidence of each theoretical claim, with direct quotes.

## I. Mechanism Coverage

### Which mechanisms appeared universally

All 8 mechanisms were found in every lesson examined. The theory has good *coverage* — nothing is absent. But frequency, form, and function vary dramatically across teachers.

**Most prevalent (found in distinctive forms in every lesson):**

| Mechanism | Universality | Variation |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **5. Constraint-as-Teacher** | Every lesson | Most varied: positional, processual, negative, temporal, social-emotional. The theory undersells this mechanism's polymorphism. |
| **6. Question as Somatic Tool** | Every lesson | Ranges from simple comparison ("is this side easier?") to philosophical provocation ("who is the heel?") to existential inquiry ("how do you know that hand is present?"). |
| **1. Naming Creates Presence** | Every lesson | From anatomical naming to developmental naming to spatial naming to naming *the significance* of a body part. |

**Most variable across teachers:**

| Mechanism | High-use teachers | Low-use teachers |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **7. Image and Metaphor** | Haller (3-5 per lesson), Strauss-Klein (sustained, warm) | Clark (nearly absent), Feldenkrais (sparse, prefers philosophical argument) |
| **2. Verb Frame** | Gibbons ("pour," "seduce," "excavated"), Haller ("invite," "observe") | Feldenkrais (predominantly imperative) |
| **4. Sensory Channel** | Strauss-Klein (multi-channel), Gibbons (muscular identification) | Most teachers stay in kinesthetic/spatial; temperature/texture absent from entire corpus |

**Systematically invisible:**
- **8. Silence/Pauses**: Cannot be evaluated from transcripts. Multiple teachers *name* rest as important ("it's a sign of intelligence"), but we cannot measure the ratio of speech to silence.

### How mechanism expression differs by teacher

| Teacher | Primary mechanisms | Signature use |
|---------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Jeff Haller** | Sensory Channel (spatial/effort), Question, Image | Anti-gravitational paradigm; floor as active partner; self-care as motor calibration |
| **Andrew Gibbons** | Constraint-as-Teacher, Naming, Question | Explicit rationale disclosure; muscular identification as perceptual target; meta-pedagogical commentary |
| **Nick Strauss-Klein** | Question, Image, Constraint | Permission-giving; aesthetic framing; social warmth in imagery; imagination as substitute for movement |
| **Scott Clark** | Question, Naming | Existential-phenomenological questions; attention-as-movement; validating absent sensation |
| **Sabine Schmid-Blackeby** | Constraint, Temporal Framing | Component-then-assembly; timing as primary teaching variable |
| **OpenATM teachers** | Constraint, Question, Image | Anti-perfectionism framing; personal anecdote as rest; differentiation as explicit instruction |
| **Moshe Feldenkrais** | Constraint, Question, Naming | Confrontation pedagogy; philosophical argument as instruction; public individual correction |
| **Feldenkrais Manhattan** | Constraint, Question, Sensory Channel | Imaginative pre-organization; clock-face as structural metaphor; quantitative sensing; agency attribution to body parts |

## II. Compositional Patterns

### Awareness Sandwich
**Status: Confirmed universal.** Found in 17/19 lessons. The two exceptions (Lips/Teeth/Mouth and Breathing Rhythmically #7) have unusual structures (lecture-demonstration and meditation-like). The scan/rescan pair is the most consistent structural element across all teachers.

### Constraint Ladder
**Status: Confirmed but needs broadening.** Found in most lessons, but the "progressive narrowing" description in the theory is too specific. Observed variations:
- **Progressive narrowing** (as described): Haller, Gibbons
- **Progressive integration** (adding components): Schmid-Blackeby (arm alone → leg alone → both)
- **Progressive complexity** (same theme, more constraints): Feldenkrais Manhattan (lift → swing → reach → grasp → open/close → clock)
- **Orientation tour** (same movement, new positions): Gibbons (back → stomach → side → sitting → elbows → head/knees)

### Effort Reduction Arc
**Status: Partially confirmed.** Clear in Haller (especially Proportional Lifting) and Strauss-Klein (Anti-Gravity). But many lessons maintain consistent effort throughout (Clark's standing lesson, Schmid-Blackeby's rolling). The theory overstates the universality of this arc.

### New compositional patterns discovered

| Pattern | Description | Where found |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Component-then-Assembly** | Parts taught separately, then combined | Schmid-Blackeby (arm, leg, both), FM Seg 7 Day 8 |
| **Attention Expansion Arc** | Progressive scope: part → region → whole self | Clark's "Come to Your Senses" |
| **Open Laboratory** | Student chooses which movements to revisit | FM Seg 3 Day 4 |
| **Reveal-and-Confront** | Attempt → look → confront gap between intention and action | Feldenkrais (Foot Differentiation) |
| **Inhibition-Release Arc** | Experience holding → mechanical opening → spontaneous release | Feldenkrais (Lips, Teeth, Mouth) |
| **Lecture-Demonstration-Practice** | Theory → demonstration → brief practice | Feldenkrais (Lips, Teeth, Mouth) |

## III. Anti-Patterns: More Complex Than Predicted

### Anti-patterns confirmed as generally harmful
- **4. Vague Sensory Language**: Confirmed. "Be aware of your body" vs. "feel the weight of your head on the floor" — the difference is consistent across all lessons.
- **5. Corrective Framing**: Confirmed in contemporary teaching. However, see Feldenkrais exception below.

### Anti-patterns that are sometimes effective

| Anti-pattern | When it works | When it doesn't |
|-------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **1. Biomechanical Lecturing** | When it deepens the meaning of movement (Feldenkrais's substitution theory, Gibbons's pedagogical philosophy) | When it replaces movement attention (lecturing *during* exploration) |
| **2. Outcome Description** | When it frames a question ("our bodies are organized to make this possible — can you find how?") | When it tells the student what to feel ("now you should feel more relaxed") |
| **3. Over-Cuing** | When it builds a detailed spatial map (Gibbons's rib-palpation sections) | When it fragments attention during continuous movement |

### Feldenkrais as special case
Moshe Feldenkrais violates nearly every anti-pattern — he lectures extensively during movement, corrects individual students publicly, and uses prescriptive framing. But his teaching is effective because:
1. His lecturing is *about* the gap between intention and action — it deepens the student's relationship to what they're discovering, not replacing it
2. His corrections demonstrate the substitution principle: "look at the difference between what you think you're doing and what you're actually doing"
3. His prescriptive framing is not "do it right" but "notice that you can't do it"

The anti-patterns need a distinction: **interrupting embodied attention** (always harmful) vs. **reframing the meaning of embodied attention** (can be powerful).

## IV. Theory Gaps: What the Corpus Reveals

### Proposed new mechanisms

#### 9. Pedagogical Transparency (Frame Disclosure)
**Claim**: Language that explains *why* the teacher is asking what they're asking fundamentally changes the student's stance toward the lesson.

**Evidence**: Present in Gibbons ("the approach is not to teach you the correct way to breathe"), Clark ("trying to reach the parts of ourselves that are actually in control"), Feldenkrais ("anything we can't do, we substitute something else for it"), Strauss-Klein ("our self-image is very personal to us").

**Effect**: Shifts the student from obedience to understanding. The student who knows *why* a constraint is imposed engages with it differently than one who simply follows instructions.

**Confidence**: High — found across 4 of 7 speaker groups, spanning historical and contemporary teachers.

#### 10. Environmental Animism (Relational Surface)
**Claim**: Language that positions the floor, gravity, or the room as active partners rather than passive surfaces changes the student's movement organization.

**Evidence**: Haller ("the floor invites you into a different story"), Strauss-Klein ("imagine the floor pushing you up"), FM Seg 7 Day 8 ("The pressure actually is distributed from a particular point and then from there"). Compare with Clark and Schmid-Blackeby who treat the floor as passive reference.

**Effect**: When the surface is animated, the student's muscular organization shifts from "pushing against" to "receiving from." This may reduce parasitic tension.

**Confidence**: Observed pattern — strong in some teachers, absent in others. May be style-specific rather than universal.

#### 11. Agency Attribution to Body Parts
**Claim**: Giving body parts their own intention, vision, or personality reorganizes proprioceptive attention.

**Evidence**: FM Seg 7 Day 8 ("the knee has an eye on it, and it's seeing a place that it's aiming towards"), Feldenkrais ("the foot just laughs at you"), Haller (floor as character). Present across historical and contemporary teachers.

**Effect**: When the knee "sees" where it's going, the student's motor planning may shift from central command to distributed organization. The body part becomes an agent rather than an instrument.

**Confidence**: Observed pattern — widespread but mechanism not independently confirmed.

#### 12. Imagination as Movement
**Claim**: Language that substitutes imagined movement for physical movement produces nervous system effects equivalent to (or complementary to) actual movement.

**Evidence**: Strauss-Klein (entire left side taught through imagination in Carriage of the Head), FM Seg 7 Day 8 ("organize it in your imagination. Get the order sensed and then go ahead"), Haller ("imagine the intention to lift").

**Effect**: Creates motor planning without execution. May be especially powerful for non-habitual movements where the student's existing motor program interferes.

**Confidence**: High — supported by motor imagery research, found across multiple teachers.

### Proposed new compositional patterns

| Pattern | Description | Confidence |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Component-then-Assembly** | Teach parts separately, then combine | High — found across multiple teachers |
| **Attention Expansion Arc** | Part → region → whole self | Observed — primarily Clark |

### Mechanism refinements needed

| Current mechanism | What the corpus shows | Proposed refinement |
|-------------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **4. Sensory Channel Selection** | "Kinesthetic" is too broad. Teachers target: weight/pressure, muscular recruitment, force-vector direction, quantitative distance, interoceptive (pulse/warmth) | Split kinesthetic into sub-channels: proprioceptive, interoceptive, effort/force, spatial-relational, quantitative |
| **6. Question as Somatic Tool** | Questions serve radically different functions | Distinguish: comparison questions, timing questions, identification questions, existential questions, philosophical questions |
| **7. Image and Metaphor** | Some teachers use extended narrative metaphors; others use quick spatial images | Distinguish: movement-organizing images, meaning-organizing narratives, developmental recapitulation |
| **5. Constraint-as-Teacher** | Constraints come in many more forms than described | Add: temporal constraints, decoupling constraints, negative constraints (defined by what NOT to do) |

## V. Speaker Differences: The Deepest Finding

### The theory of how learning happens differs by teacher

| Teacher | Theory of learning | Primary linguistic tool |
|---------|-------------------|------------------------|
| **Haller** | Change the body image and the movement follows | Spatial-perceptual imagery, anti-gravitational framing |
| **Gibbons** | Understand the mechanics and the movement becomes available | Anatomical precision, constraint rationale, meta-commentary |
| **Strauss-Klein** | Create the right conditions (permission, curiosity, safety) and the nervous system self-organizes | Questions, permissions, warm imagery |
| **Clark** | Direct attention precisely and let sensation emerge | Existential questions, radical compression, naming |
| **Feldenkrais** | Confront the gap between intention and action | Provocation, philosophical argument, public demonstration |
| **FM teachers** | Build precise spatial-temporal maps through systematic variation | Clock-face structures, quantitative sensing, imaginative pre-organization |

This variation is the most important finding. The seed theory describes mechanisms as if they are neutral tools that any teacher deploys. In practice, each teacher has an implicit **theory of learning** that determines which mechanisms they emphasize and how they deploy them. The booklet's explanatory layer should address this: the same mechanisms serve different pedagogical philosophies.

### Historical vs. contemporary language

| Dimension | Feldenkrais (1970s) | Contemporary teachers |
|-----------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| **Verb frame** | Imperative dominant | Invitational dominant |
| **Correction** | Direct, public | Absent or indirect |
| **Theory** | Integrated into instruction | Separated or meta-commented |
| **Humor** | Ironic, confrontational | Self-deprecating, warm |
| **Student agency** | Teacher-directed | Student-empowered |
| **Treatment of failure** | Confrontation ("the foot laughs at you") | Normalization ("it's a sign of intelligence to rest") |

## VII. Next Steps

1. **Update seed theory**: Add 4 new mechanisms, 2 new compositional patterns, refine 4 existing mechanisms, add anti-pattern nuance
2. **Reclassify confidence levels**: Several "High confidence" claims need qualifying ("High but varies by teacher")
3. **Flag for human review**: The historical vs. contemporary divergence, especially around anti-patterns (is Feldenkrais's confrontational style effective or outdated?)
4. **Booklet implication**: The explanatory layer must address *why* teachers differ, not just *how* — the implicit theory of learning is the key variable
